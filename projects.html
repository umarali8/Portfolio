Content Refresh Prioritization

Shipped

FlyRank AI/ML Internship — end-to-end ML capstone

Problem

SEO and content teams need a data-driven way to decide which of thousands of webpages to prioritize for a content refresh, instead of relying on gut feel or manual review.

What I Built

I designed and built the full pipeline solo: data cleaning, feature engineering, leakage detection, model training, and evaluation. I started with a rule-based baseline, then developed an interpretable Decision Tree classifier, benchmarked against that baseline for a fair comparison.

Approach

Analyzed 15 search-performance and content features — including content age, impressions, average position, CTR, and word count — across 30,000 anonymized webpage records from 32 clients, using client-holdout validation to keep results honest across clients.

Accuracy — Baseline vs. Model (Test Set)
Outcome

The Decision Tree reached 87.17% accuracy on held-out clients — and the same framework showed the simple baseline was actually competitive at 87.93%, a more honest and useful finding than shipping unnecessary complexity. impressions_90d emerged as the dominant predictive signal.

Taking the Model Public

Shipped

Case Study 2 — publishing the page-priority model as an inspectable research paper

Problem

The page-priority model was built and tested, but it was sitting in notebooks — nobody outside the project could see it, check the work, or judge whether the reasoning held up. A model nobody can inspect isn't finished, it's just private.

What I Built

I turned the internship dataset and model work into a public research paper — the question, the data, the baseline, the decision tree, the leakage check, and what's still pending — deployed live, with the DNS setup documented in advance, then sent out for a real design review.

From Notebook to Public Paper
Approach

No claims dressed up past what the evaluation supports — the paper says plainly that the baseline-vs-model comparison isn't final until client-holdout validation is complete. From the design review, I fixed what actually blocked understanding: a broken link in the reproducibility section, and a mobile nav that disappeared entirely instead of just shrinking.

Outcome

The paper is live, the repo is public, and the reasoning is inspectable end to end — problem, data, method, results, limits, and next steps.

The Refresh Loop

Shipped

AI Fluency capstone — connecting the model, the paper, and the voice into one repeatable system

Problem

Three real pieces of work already existed side by side — a model that scores what needs attention, a paper that explains it honestly, and a consistent writing voice — but they didn't talk to each other. Turning finished work into a case study was a manual, one-off conversation every time.

What I Built

A closed loop connecting all three pieces: a documented AI workflow with human sign-off points, a quality checklist, a prompt library, and the new piece — a Case Study Intake Agent, a small Claude Project that interviews a raw piece of work one question at a time and drafts a case study in the same three-beat shape, flagging any claim bigger than what's actually proven.

The Loop, Closed
Approach

Scoped the agent to have a defined job, output shape, and a built-in guardrail — not just a chat wrapper — and kept it simple enough to actually finish and reuse next month. Also produced a standalone Capstone Review Packet PDF so a reviewer can read the whole paper without needing live-site or repo access.

Outcome

Shipped all three pieces: the workflow doc, a live "How I work" section on this portfolio, and a working Case Study Intake Agent. The loop now closes end to end — next project goes through the agent, the agent drafts the case study, and the workflow doc stays proven by use rather than by claim alone.
